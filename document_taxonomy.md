# Document Taxonomy

This taxonomy groups document genres by their **primary reader job**: what a reader needs the document to help them do. It is deliberately not a mutually exclusive filing system. A single document can combine genres (for example, a proposal may contain an executive summary, a case study, an appendix, and a FAQ).

Use the primary job to select a document type; use audience, medium, length, and placement as secondary labels. Terms such as *one-pager*, *appendix*, and *index* describe a format or location rather than a purpose, so they are listed separately.

## 1. Orient and establish shared understanding

| Document | Primary reader job | Typical characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Overview** | Understand the scope, context, and major components of a topic, project, or product. | Broad, selective, and often links to deeper material; may include milestones, but a timeline is not required. |
| **Primer / 101** | Learn the foundations of an unfamiliar subject. | Explains core concepts, vocabulary, and mental models; the level of jargon depends on the intended beginner. |
| **Backgrounder** | Gain concise context on an issue, organization, event, or person. | Factual, selective, and usually written for a defined audience such as media, stakeholders, or decision-makers. |
| **Fact sheet** | Retrieve verified key facts quickly. | Short, highly scannable, and sourced as appropriate; may use statistics, but need not be visually promotional. |
| **FAQ** | Resolve recurring questions and objections. | Question-and-answer structure; can support onboarding, support, policy, or sales. |

## 2. Condense and brief

| Document | Primary reader job | Typical characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Summary** | Understand the main points of one source without reading it in full. | Neutral and proportionate to the source; may follow its structure or organize by theme. |
| **Executive summary** | Understand the decision, rationale, findings, implications, and requested action quickly. | Written for senior stakeholders; often includes the recommendation and key evidence. It may briefly state methodology when credibility or interpretation depends on it. |
| **Brief** | Prepare for a specific decision, task, meeting, or communication. | Scoped to an audience and use case; typically covers context, objectives, constraints, options, and/or asks. A brief does not always prescribe action. |
| **Digest** | Stay current across multiple items over a period of time. | Curated, modular aggregation with links or citations back to the underlying sources. |
| **Abstract** | Decide whether a scholarly or technical work is relevant enough to read. | Concise and self-contained; length and structure vary by venue. Research abstracts often cover purpose, methods, results, and conclusions, but not all use a fixed IMRaD structure. |
| **Précis** | Preserve a work’s essential argument and progression in compressed form. | More faithful to the source’s reasoning and tone than a general summary; generally introduces no independent critique. |

## 3. Inform decisions and persuade

| Document | Primary reader job | Typical characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Decision memo** | Make or approve a defined decision. | States the decision needed, options, recommendation, trade-offs, evidence, owner, and timing. |
| **Proposal** | Evaluate and approve a future course of action. | Defines the problem or opportunity, proposed approach, scope, benefits, cost, risks, and next steps. |
| **Business case** | Judge whether an investment or initiative is worthwhile. | Compares expected benefits, costs, risks, assumptions, alternatives, and financial or strategic impact. |
| **Policy memo** | Decide on a public, organizational, or operational policy position. | Frames the issue, analyzes options and consequences, and recommends a policy action. |
| **White paper** | Understand a complex issue, technology, or approach in depth. | Researched and evidence-led; may educate, analyze, or advocate. It is not inherently promotional, nor must it promote one organization’s solution. |
| **Position paper** | Understand and assess a stated viewpoint. | Makes a defensible claim, supports it with evidence and reasoning, and addresses material counterarguments. |
| **Case study** | Learn from a bounded real-world situation. | Documents context, intervention or decision, evidence, outcomes, and lessons. It can examine failure, mixed results, or non-commercial outcomes as well as success. |
| **Review** | Evaluate the quality, suitability, or performance of something. | Uses explicit criteria and evidence; may be subjective, comparative, or recommendatory depending on the domain. |

## 4. Research, analyze, and report

| Document | Primary reader job | Typical characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Report** | Understand findings from an investigation, activity, or period of work. | Usually records purpose, evidence, analysis, findings, and conclusions; recommendations are optional. |
| **Research paper / article** | Assess an original scholarly contribution. | Follows disciplinary conventions, documents methods and evidence, and makes claims that can be evaluated or reproduced where applicable. |
| **Literature review** | Understand the state of knowledge across existing sources. | Synthesizes themes, agreements, disagreements, gaps, and source quality; it is not merely a list of summaries. |
| **Assessment / evaluation** | Judge performance, impact, compliance, or maturity against criteria. | Defines the framework, evidence, ratings or findings, limitations, and improvement actions where relevant. |
| **Postmortem / incident report** | Understand what happened, why, and how recurrence will be reduced. | Time-bounded account of impact, timeline, contributing factors, corrective actions, owners, and follow-up. |

## 5. Specify, govern, and record commitments

| Document | Primary reader job | Typical characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Requirements document** | Agree on what a product, service, or project must achieve. | Defines user, business, functional, non-functional, and acceptance requirements; separates requirements from implementation where possible. |
| **Specification** | Implement or verify a system consistently. | Precise, testable description of interfaces, behavior, constraints, data, and edge cases. |
| **Standard / policy** | Establish rules that people or systems are expected to follow. | Identifies scope, obligations, exceptions, enforcement, and ownership. A policy sets intent; a standard usually supplies more prescriptive rules. |
| **Contract / agreement** | Record enforceable commitments between parties. | Defines parties, obligations, terms, risks, remedies, and governing conditions; requires appropriate legal review. |
| **Charter** | Authorize and frame an initiative, team, or body. | States purpose, scope, authority, stakeholders, decision rights, and success measures. |
| **Meeting minutes** | Preserve decisions, actions, and salient discussion from a meeting. | Records attendees, decisions, action owners, deadlines, and unresolved items; not a verbatim transcript by default. |

## 6. Enable execution and support

| Document | Primary reader job | Typical characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Guide / manual** | Complete a task or use a product successfully. | Explanatory, usually task-oriented, and may include concepts, procedures, examples, and troubleshooting. |
| **Tutorial** | Build capability through a guided learning sequence. | Teaches by doing, assumes a starting level, and usually produces a concrete outcome. |
| **Standard operating procedure (SOP)** | Perform a repeatable process consistently. | Defines purpose, scope, roles, prerequisites, ordered steps, controls, and records. |
| **Runbook** | Operate, diagnose, or restore a live system. | Operationally specific, time-sensitive, and includes commands, checks, escalation paths, and rollback or recovery steps. |
| **Playbook** | Respond effectively to recurring scenarios. | Combines decision criteria, roles, strategies, and reusable actions. Flowcharts are common but not required. |
| **Checklist** | Avoid omissions during a bounded task. | Short, verifiable items; complements rather than replaces detailed instruction when judgment is needed. |
| **Troubleshooting guide** | Diagnose and resolve known problems. | Symptom-to-cause paths, checks, remedies, and escalation guidance. |

## 7. Reference and retrieval aids

| Document | Primary reader job | Typical characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Glossary** | Interpret specialized terms consistently. | Defines selected terms for a subject, organization, or document set; often alphabetized but not required to be. |
| **Reference guide** | Look up stable facts, commands, rules, or interfaces during work. | Optimized for retrieval rather than sequential reading; can be organized by task or concept rather than alphabetically. |
| **Technical/API reference** | Integrate with a system correctly. | Documents endpoints or interfaces, parameters, schemas, behavior, errors, examples, versioning, and limits. |
| **Cheat sheet** | Recall high-frequency commands, formulas, syntax, or patterns rapidly. | Intentionally compressed; useful to experienced users but insufficient as standalone instruction for most beginners. |
| **Directory / catalog** | Find and select resources, people, products, or records. | Searchable or browsable listings with standardized fields and navigation. |

## 8. Format, placement, and maintenance labels

These labels can be applied to documents in any category above; they should not be treated as competing genres.

| Label | What it denotes | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **One-pager** | A document constrained to one page. | May be an overview, proposal, fact sheet, or brief. A call to action is optional. |
| **Appendix / annex** | Supplementary material attached to a main document. | Can contain methods, evidence, definitions, calculations, forms, or data. It is not necessarily raw or non-essential. *Annex* often denotes material that is more formally integral. |
| **Index** | A navigation aid locating concepts in a larger work. | Traditionally uses page numbers; digital versions may use anchors, links, or search metadata. |
| **Template** | A reusable structure for producing a type of document. | Encodes sections, prompts, and placeholders rather than serving as a completed genre itself. |
| **Form** | A structured instrument for collecting standardized inputs. | Used for requests, approvals, assessments, records, and transactions. |
| **Changelog / release notes** | A record or explanation of changes over versions. | A changelog is usually exhaustive and chronological; release notes are curated for affected users. |

## Selection guide

Choose by the reader’s next action:

| If the reader needs to… | Start with… |
| --- | --- |
| Get oriented | Overview, primer, backgrounder, or fact sheet |
| Decide or approve | Decision memo, proposal, business case, or policy memo |
| Evaluate a claim or outcome | Report, research paper, assessment, review, or case study |
| Build or operate something | Requirements, specification, SOP, runbook, guide, or playbook |
| Find an answer while working | Reference guide, technical reference, glossary, cheat sheet, or FAQ |
| Scan a longer work first | Abstract, executive summary, summary, or précis |

When a document has several goals, name the primary genre first and add descriptive labels, e.g. “one-page decision brief,” “technical reference guide,” or “incident postmortem with appendix.”
